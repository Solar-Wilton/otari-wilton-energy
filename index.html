Could Otari-Wilton become a community energy pilot?

We’re exploring solar, battery options, neighbourhood energy savings and possible small-scale wind feasibility for community sites and nearby homes around Otari-Wilton.

Why we’re starting this

We’re Sam and Allan. We recently moved into our forever home on Wilton Road after living in Wilton for the past three years, having originally moved here from the Waikato.

We love this area — the bush, the community, the history, and the feeling of being tucked into one of Wellington’s most special neighbourhoods.

1

Making our home more sustainable

We’re rejuvenating our home and want to do it in a way that lowers our energy use, reduces our power bills and makes better use of renewable energy.

2

Solar can be expensive alone

Like many people, we quickly found that solar panels and batteries can involve a significant upfront cost, especially if you want a system that makes a real difference.

3

What if we explored it together?

Instead of every household solving this alone, we’re exploring whether a community-led approach could help nearby homes, community facilities and local organisations.

4

A pilot other communities could copy

Otari-Wilton has a unique mix of homes, community spaces and local identity. If this works here, it could become a model for other Wellington suburbs.

The idea

This is not a fixed proposal yet. The first step is simply to find out whether local residents, community organisations, Council and energy partners are interested in exploring a practical community energy project.

🏠

Neighbourhood bulk-buy

Interested homes could explore group pricing for solar, battery-ready systems or battery storage.

🔋

Community buildings

Suitable local facilities could investigate solar and batteries to lower operating costs and support sustainable energy use.

🌬️

Wind feasibility

Because Wilton is hilly and windy, small-scale wind could be investigated as an add-on if it stacks up.

Why Otari-Wilton?

The area has a rare mix of community facilities, nearby homes, bush reserve identity and a strong case for a locally led sustainability project.

✓

Community anchor sites nearby

Potential links with the community centre, preschool, lawn bowls club and other local organisations.

✓

Lower energy costs

Community facilities and households could explore ways to reduce energy costs and make renewable energy more accessible.

✓

Replicable pilot

If this works here, the model could be shared with other Wellington suburbs.

What happens next?

The project will only move forward if there is enough community interest and the technical, legal and financial details make sense.

Gather local interest

Collect names from residents, community groups and potential anchor sites.

Speak to key partners

Approach Council, community organisations, Wellington Electricity and solar/battery installers.

Request early feasibility advice

Check likely system sizes, battery options, costs, network limits and whether wind is worth exploring.

Share the findings

Report back to interested locals before any decisions or commitments are made.
